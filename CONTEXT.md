Brasaland is a chain of grilled cuisine restaurants founded in 2008 in Medellín, Colombia. What began as a single family store became, in fifteen years, a chain of 14 own restaurants that operates in two countries: Colombia and the United States (Florida). The company employs about 115 people and has a turnover of around 6 million dollars per year.

The brand has always stood for three things: a meal that tastes the same regardless of whether you order it in Medellin or Miami, a warm and consistent service experience, and a cuisine that moves fast. Those three commitments are what built the business — and they're also what make running it increasingly difficult without the right tools.

How the company is organized
Brasaland operates from its headquarters in Medellín. There is a commercial and operations office in Miami that coordinates the Florida locations. The company is led by Mariana Restrepo, daughter of the founder and CEO since 2019. It was she who brought the business to the US market and is now focused on building the internal systems that allow it to continue growing without losing what makes the brand good.

The company is organized into the following areas:

Restaurant Operations is the heart of the business. Felipe Guerrero leads a team of supervisors who oversee the 14 locations in the two countries. Every day, each location manages its own kitchen, its own team and its own orders for raw materials, largely without visibility from the headquarters.

Purchasing & Suppliers manages relationships with the approximately 20 suppliers who supply everything from meat and vegetables to packaging and cleaning products. Lucía Fernández carries out this function, negotiating independently in each market without consolidated data on what the chain is really spending.

Marketing & Digital Experience is responsible for how Brasaland presents itself to its customers: the web, social media, loyalty program and campaigns. Camila Ospina leads the team. The brand exists in two culturally distinct markets, and for now, there is virtually no data on who Brasaland's customers are.

People and Culture manages everything related to the 115 people who work in the 14 premises: contracts, schedules, onboarding and day-to-day HR. Ashley Turner leads this role from Miami and works daily with the complexity of two very different labor markets.

Training and Quality Standards is the area where Jake Morrison works to ensure that a Brasaland burger is prepared and presented the same way in every kitchen. Training materials exist, but they are difficult to consult and even more difficult to keep up to date in two countries.

Technology is led by CTO Nicolás Park, based in Medellín and with a small team. The current tech stack is minimal: a static website, an outdated app, different point-of-sale systems in each country, and a collection of spreadsheets that have been doing the work of real management systems for years.

Executive management falls to Mariana, who currently makes most of the strategic decisions based on phone calls, weekly PDF reports that arrive on Tuesday mornings, and her own experience of the business.

Where is the company today
Brasaland is profitable and has a loyal customer base in both markets. But he's managing a two-country operation with tools designed for a single local restaurant. The consequences are visible: raw material orders made via WhatsApp with no inventory data behind it, a loyalty program that works with physical stamp cards that customers constantly miss, no real-time visibility into what's going on at each location, and a management team that can't answer basic business questions without making calls.

Meanwhile, competitors — including newer and smaller chains — are gaining traction with digital ordering, data-driven marketing and operational dashboards that allow their managers to act on real information rather than intuition.

Mariana has created an internal team called Brasaland Digital to change this. The mandate is to build the tools, systems and automations that allow Brasaland to operate as a modern company without losing the soul of what it has always been.

You are part of that team.

Departments and their problems
🍖 Restaurant Operations
Director: Felipe Guerrero

Each of the 14 locations operates largely in isolation. There is no centralized visibility: Felipe does not know in real time how many covers were served today at the downtown Medellín location or if the Miami restaurant is having a weak week. Ingredient orders are made via WhatsApp or phone, which causes excess stock in some stores and breakages in others. Shift reports are filled out on paper or Excel and sent to HR on a weekly basis.

What they need: A real-time sales dashboard by location (in COP and USD), an intelligent ingredient ordering system based on historical sales and current stock, and automatic alerts when a store does not register sales during opening hours.

🛒 Purchasing and suppliers
Responsible: Lucía Fernández

Brasaland works with around 20 suppliers between Colombia and Florida — meat, vegetables, sauces, beverages, packaging, cleaning products. Price negotiation is done by email and Excel. Lucía learns about the changes in the price of raw materials when the bill arrives. There is no consolidated data on purchases at the chain level.

What they need: A supplier management platform with price history and alerts, and consolidated purchasing visibility to facilitate centralized negotiations in both markets.

📱 Marketing and digital experience
Responsible: Camila Ospina

Brasaland's website is from 2019, does not accept online orders and has a rating of 2.8 in the app store. The loyalty program ("Brasa Points") works with physical stamp cards — 60% of customers do not use them and the cards do not generate any data. There is no information about who Brasaland's customers really are.

What they need: A digital loyalty and ordering app, a customer CRM with order history and preferences, and a personalization engine that suggests relevant products based on behavior.

🧑 🤝 🧑 People and culture
Responsible: Ashley Turner (Miami)

Managing 115 people in 14 locations in two countries with very different labour laws creates a huge administrative burden. Most HR processes are managed by email and Excel. The incorporation of new kitchen staff — with high turnover — is completely manual.

What they need: An internal HR portal for vacation requests and absence management, an automated onboarding flow, and a dashboard of HR KPIs with turnover, absenteeism, and job fill times — segmented by country.

🎓 Training and quality standards
Responsible: Jake Morrison (Miami)

All venues must follow the same recipes, preparation techniques, and presentation standards regardless of the country. The training materials are on a shared Google Drive that no one knows how to navigate. When a prescription or procedure changes, communicating the update to all 14 locations in two languages takes days and often leads to confusion.

What they need: A training platform with a searchable recipe catalog, a structured onboarding itinerary for new staff, and a system that distributes recipe updates to all locations simultaneously. Multi-language support (Spanish and English) is optional but highly recommended, starting with a base language.

💻 Technology
CTO: Nicolás Park (Medellín)

Brasaland's current tech stack is minimal: a static website, an outdated app, a different point-of-sale terminal in each country without integration, and spreadsheets that act as management systems. There is no internal API, no consolidated data, and no telemetry. Nicolás has the mandate to build Brasaland's digital platform from almost scratch.

What they need: A central Brasaland API covering premises, menus, sales, customers and suppliers; real-time telemetry from each location; and a data pipeline that feeds operations, marketing, and finance dashboards.

📊 Executive management
CEO: Mariana Restrepo

Mariana manages a chain of 14 stores in two markets without a unified dashboard. Your decisions are based on calls with the operations team, PDF reports that arrive on Tuesdays, and intuition. You can't answer in real time: "how much did we sell this week in Florida?" or "which store has the highest average ticket this month?"

What you need: An executive dashboard with the chain's total sales in USD and COP, an AI assistant that you can consult in natural language, and an automated weekly report generated and sent every Monday at 7 am.

AI challenges at Brasaland range from ingredient demand prediction and menu customization to multilingual support agents and real-time sales telemetry. If you want to build systems whose value a non-technical person understands immediately, Brasaland is your company.
